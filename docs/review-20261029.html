3. Why did 0.2778 score highest? (analysis, not a receipt)

  1. It is a pruned member of one family. The 0.2778 file (37,654 dots) has 100.0% of its dots within 3 px of GEMSDOE25's 44,090 dots and 100.0% of GEMSDOE24's. Every focus file in the table has 98.1%–100.0% of its dots within 3 px of the H19-5 powerlaw raster (121,131 dots). The README's B-pruning sequence (H19-5 → D2.8 → B=1 → B=2) is the claimed lineage; the overlap numbers are verified, the lineage itself is the README's claim.
  2. Hypothesis — pruning removes wasted dots, not structure. Under the metric, a dot counts as a false positive unless it is within 300 m of a withheld fault. Dots on already-mapped USGS traces are plausibly less likely to lie within 300 m of an unmapped fault, so removing them may lower false-positive mass. The surviving dots keep most of the recall. This is untested here; the holdout does not measure it directly.
  3. The repo's HIDE-RECOVER holdout does not predict the leaderboard here. The 0.2778 file scores HIDE-RECOVER 0.0045 (rank 36 of 53 scored rasters). It is 13 of 53 on SGMC-CAL. The ordering across the five focus files is Spearman ρ = +0.90 for SGMC-CAL and -0.60 for HIDE-RECOVER (n = 5, indicative only).

So the answer to "can we beat 0.2778?" is not yet demonstrable here. The only instrument that orders the focus files roughly like the leaderboard is SGMC-CAL, and it differs by about 0.01 between files, with overlapping CIs. Beating 0.2778 needs a new family that is not a subset of H19-5, plus a receipt from a real submission.
