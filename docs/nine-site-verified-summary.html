2. Why 0.2778 scored highest (PhD-level reading of the measured evidence)

What the evidence shows (MEASURED-HERE):

  • The champion has 37,654 dots, the fewest of the nine files, and 0.0% of them lie within 200 m of a catalogued fault (labels.tif). Its dots lie within 3 px of the other focus files' dots at 0.999–1.000 (GEMSDOE24, 25, 27, 28, 30, 31, 33, 36).
  • Across the nine owner-reported files, live score falls with dot count (Spearman ρ = -0.983) and with the share of dots near catalogued faults (ρ = -0.894). The two files with 0% near-catalogue dots that have live scores (GEMSDOE32 and GEMSDOE36) are the top two live scores in the family. H51-1 has the same 0% share but no live score.

Mechanism (metric algebra, from src/gems32/metric.py; DTI = TP/(α(TP+FP)+β|G|), α=0.2, β=0.8): a dot raises DTI only if its kernel-weighted credit k exceeds α·DTI. At DTI ≈ 0.28 that threshold is k ≈ 0.056. Dots that are near catalogued faults and carry little credit against the scored truth therefore cost more than they earn. Pruning them (the B-gate ladder) raises DTI. This requires the scored truth to lie mainly off-catalogue. The repo cites a known-fault masking clarification for that point, but it was not re-read this session. This is the best-supported explanation, and it is an inference, not a measured result.

Confound: in this family dot mass and catalogue share move together, so n=9 cannot separate them. Partial OLS gives both negative coefficients, but the data cannot say which one drives the score. A clean test needs two files of the same mass that differ only in catalogue share, which is the H51-1 experiment below.

Named non-fault mimic: basin-fill sparsity. A file whose dots sit in mapped-fault-free basin fill (alluvium over smooth gravity and magnetic fields) would show 0% catalogue proximity without detecting any fault. The same live signal could then come from avoiding catalogue-adjacent terrain rather than from finding faults.

5. Why no literal uniqueness pass is possible for any dense file

r13-lattice-s5 (206,895 dots, a registry raster with an OWNER-REPORT score of 0.0904) lies within 3 px of 99.87% of the footprint. Only 6,649 footprint pixels lie outside its collar. Under the 70% rule a file needs at least 30% of its dots outside that collar, so it can hold at most 22,163 dots. Any file that meets the rule would therefore be a sparse file placed mostly on the leftover pixels. That is rule-satisfaction, not geology, so it is not proposed. The repo's own figure (6,514 px and a 21,713-dot bound, after known-fault exclusion) differs from this raw count; both are logged (IR-20261029-V04). Owner decision needed: exempt dense calibration rasters, or replace the rule with a density-aware test.
